In the same narrow rocky tide channel, foam climbs between dark wet rocks under a storm sky. On the lower rock a small dark seabird is still alive and tangled in thin metal wire — a storm-petrel, the tubenose with tiny nostrils on the bill. A four-legged fox stays cut off on the rocks, free of the wire but unable to reach high ground. Rustboy — the leftover scrap kid with one green eye and a sealed rust face — leaves the safer high rock. He steps down into the channel water and crouches over the bird, looking down at it.

That is the first move of help, not a finished rescue. He has been hunting a purpose through empty answers and quiet company; here he chooses to help at a cost — wet feet in rising water, the fox still stranded, the wire still holding the bird. The film’s help climax opens on this step. Unweaving the wire comes later; this post stops at the crouch.
