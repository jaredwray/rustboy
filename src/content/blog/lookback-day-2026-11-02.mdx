Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is a little way down the trail now, out on the open headland in gray daylight, wind and salt mist. Behind him, back across the slope, stands the keep: the dark, spired fortress where he woke up and climbed all the way to the top. From here it looks small. Its arched door is dark, and two lamps are still burning on either side of it. His body is already pointed on down the trail, but his head has turned back over his shoulder toward it.

Why that matters: nobody made him leave. The place that made him simply had no job for him, so going is his own choice. This is the one moment he lets himself see what he's leaving behind. Nothing in the keep calls him back. In the story, he turns and keeps walking.

## One new shot

There's one new shot in today's cut. Until now the film went from Rustboy setting off along the outdoor path straight to the rain further on, and nobody ever saw him look back. This shot sits between those two, at about 2:13 to 2:19 in the cut.

## How we made it

The approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) has no drawing of him looking over his shoulder. So we built the pose from two of its views, both used exactly as drawn and never flipped. His body comes from one view, standing and facing on down the trail. His head comes from another, turned back toward the keep. The only change is the turn at the neck. That keeps his green eye on the viewer's left and his empty socket on the other side, as always.

That choice has a cost we decided to accept. To keep his face and eye toward the camera, his gaze points toward the side of the frame where the keep stands rather than straight back into the distance at its door. A truer look into the distance would have shown only the back of his head.

The keep in the background isn't new. It's the same fortress already seen in the path shot, scaled down and set far back on its headland so it matches what's already in the film. Our first version used a different, plainer tower, and we threw it out because it didn't match. The two lamps by the door come from that same earlier shot, and we kept them lit so the two shots agree.

As with the last few shots, we locked the look of the still first, then grew the clip from it with only the surroundings moving. Our quality check compared every frame against that still, and not one of Rustboy's pixels, or the keep's, changed in any frame.
