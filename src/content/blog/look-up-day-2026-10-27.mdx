Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone deep inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. He stands at the foot of a tall spiral service stair — a stone-and-steel shaft of pale god rays and drifting rust motes. The film calls this place the well of light; it is not a water well. He stops and looks straight up the whole column before the first step, half-hoping purpose is waiting up there. He is tiny in the shaft on purpose. Only the room moves: the god rays shimmer a little, and dust motes drift.

Why that matters: he woke up with no purpose and has been trying every answer the keep offers. The dead phone gave him a "job" that went nowhere, the light behind the glass didn't pair with him, he left the big caged lever alone, and the badges on the wall were not his face. The stair is the way up toward the door of light. Looking up is the hope that purpose might still be waiting above — before the climb (and the later mid-stair sag) teach him that no assignment is coming to meet him. He takes the stairs anyway.

## How we made it

The finished still is him tipped back looking straight up, green optic on the viewer-left side of his head, whole uncut limbs, solid rather than skinny. He's built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) as drawn, never flipped or stretched: one green eye, dark empty socket, nose, mouth slit, chest port, rounded feet. We kept him tiny in the shaft on purpose so the column of light reads tall. The still and clip both passed QC (our frame-by-frame check against that sheet); the clip's first frame matches the still exactly, and Rustboy — including his optic — doesn't change in a single frame. We locked the look before it went into motion. The clip is grown from the finished still with environment-only motion: the room breathes while he stays still on purpose.

The new shot went into the cut from the full-quality master copy of the clip.

## What it sounds like

The visitor-corridor air continues through this look-up, with a thin well-air hint and a soft ray shimmer. The film's little searching tune is held back on purpose — he has not found a purpose yet, so no theme celebrating one. He's frozen, so there are no footstep or body sounds. The new shot sits at about −26.3 LUFS (a loudness measure), against about −21.0 LUFS for the whole cut, with a true peak of −7.4 dBTP.
