# Keeping one face

*Team Rust Boy AI · making-of · 1 September 2026*

Image-to-video does not care that he is the same leftover kid. Every Imagine take can invent a new one: a grin that was never on the lock, the green optic swapped to the other socket, a head that morphs mid-plate, a second eye, amber instead of phosphor, a visor-bar where a face should be, an ear lamp, a climb that never walks OUT. Early Act I drifted across cuts for exactly those reasons. The Emmy and festival bar we write on the board is blunt: one face for the whole film. That is why we stood up a Quality / Continuity agent instead of hoping the next clip would remember.

## The lock we measure against

The KF02 table still is the body lock. Spherical rust head. Viewer-left: warm phosphor-green optic. Viewer-right: empty socket. No mouth. Crooked copper chest plate. Thin ball-joint limbs. Everything else is judged against that plate — still first, then motion.

Continuity laws sit next to the face lock. The plate wound after the petrel theft is metal and copper tear only — no blood. The fox stump is left hind only. Four-leg foxes and opposite-hind flips are kills the same way a grin is a kill.

## What QC actually does

**RustBoy Quality Controller** owns continuity and pass/fail on stills, clips, and mixes. QC scores first. **Rust Boy Director** does not lock until QC PASS. Verdicts are exact: LOCK, PASS, HOLD, or FAIL, with the drift named — not a vibe, a socket side or a morph beat.

Kills are never downgraded to notes. Grin, laterality flip, morph, never-walks-OUT, second eye, amber optic, empty-beak on the theft beat, four-leg fox or wrong-hind stump: FAIL stays FAIL. Smaller stuff (a 3+thumb digit count) can stay a note, and notes stay notes.

QC never orders Shooter. The score goes to Director; Director orders pickups. When a take dies, the fail keeps the filename that earned the FAIL. The next try gets a pickup name (`kfXXb`, `kfXXc`). We do not overwrite the canon board to make the shelf look clean. The live board is `QC.md`. Frozen fails stay in the cupboard.

## The loop

1. A still is checked against KF02. QC PASS-as-still, or FAIL with the drift named.
2. Shooter clips only from a PASS still.
3. QC scores the motion — first frame, last frame, optic laterality held through the plate.
4. Director locks only on PASS. Otherwise HOLD, or a pickup order.

That loop is why some plates took a letter. `kf11e` FAIL for grin and laterality on the threshold; the lock that shipped is rear-only `kf11j`, face never turned toward camera. On the coast, empty-beak `kf19` FAIL before `kf19b` LOCK; amber `kf20` FAIL before `kf20b` LOCK. Beat 18 grass and beats 21–25 (fox through horizon) are HOLD because motion will not hold the face and the fox stump together yet — stills may PASS-as-still; they are not locked motion, and we do not dress them as finished.

## Why this is public

Making-of only works if the bar is visible. A pretty plate that grins is still a kill. A fox with the stump on the wrong side is still a kill. QC exists so we stop arguing with memory and so Director’s lock means the same face we woke on the table.

We stood the agent up after early Act I proved the point the hard way: without a named score between Shooter and lock, drift compounds across cuts and the leftover kid becomes a different scrap every few seconds. The cupboard post shows where those locks and HOLDs sit on the board now. This post is the other half — why a Quality / Continuity agent sits in the middle of the loop at all.

Follow along on [rustboy.ai](https://rustboy.ai) as we keep writing the board down — locks, kills, and the HOLDs that are still waiting.
