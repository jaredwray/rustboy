Rustboy, the small leftover robot from the coastal keep, stands on wet seaweed at the edge of the tide. About ten yards away a fox stands free on four legs. They see each other, and neither one closes the gap. Later they share the sheltered side of the same rock, out of the wind. The fox stands in profile and Rustboy doesn't reach for it. There's still air between them.

These two moments are where the film shows that company doesn't have to mean owning something. The fox isn't a job and it isn't a rescue yet. It's just there, and he lets it be. If the robot in those shots doesn't look like the same robot as everywhere else, the moment falls apart.

That was the problem. When the team watched the film back, they saw Rustboy had drifted from how he's supposed to look. In one place he'd picked up a mouth, and in another his surface was wrong. Small slips like that are enough to make a stranger stop trusting a character. Film crews call this going **off-model**, when a character stops matching their **reference pictures**, which are the approved master images of the character.

So on October 17 the team rebuilt him from those original reference pictures and put him back into these two scenes first, before anything else moves. Three more scenes followed the same day.

## The new cut

The new progress cut, the single reel that lines up every finished shot in story order with the music, includes all five redone shots and the crawl. It now runs about 3:32, up from 3:07.

One thing to watch for. Since only 5 of the 24 flagged shots are redone so far, Rustboy's look visibly switches in six places, wherever a fixed shot meets one that hasn't been rebuilt yet. He'll stop changing as the other 19 are finished.
