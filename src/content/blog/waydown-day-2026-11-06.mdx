Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is no longer at the top of the cliff. He has come partway down the headland and is standing on a wet rock shelf in the same gray light after the rain, with salt mist and wind around him. Ahead of him is the ledge where the storm-petrel landed with the copper flake it pecked off his chest. The bird is still there, the flake with it. His head is tilted slightly toward it, and his green eye is on the ledge. He hasn't reached it yet.

Why that matters: in the last scene he stood at the cliff top and watched where the bird went. Now he's lower, which means he chose to follow it. Without this shot the film jumped straight from the cliff top to the nest, and arriving at the nest felt like a cut. Now it feels like his decision.

## One new shot

There's one new shot in today's cut, between the cliff top and the nest. In our last post we said plainly that the film didn't yet show how he got from the cliff top down to the nest. This shot partly fills that gap. He's now seen partway down, on the shelf. It only partly fills it, though: the climb itself still isn't in the film. The cut goes from him standing at the cliff top straight to him already on the shelf.

## How we made it

Rustboy is built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks), used as drawn and never flipped. As in the cliff-top shot, his body comes from one of the sheet's three-quarter views and his head from another, turned and tilted slightly toward the ledge. That's the only acting. He doesn't crouch, lean or reach, and his hands stay at his sides. The petrel is the same bird from the shots around it, and it hasn't moved.

We made more than one version of the still. The figure looked right in all of them, but in some the sky was noticeably brighter than in the shots before and after, which would have jumped at each cut. We picked the version whose sky matched its neighbours, so the gray afternoon carries straight through.

The motion needed one adjustment too. Our quality check found the grass close to him was moving a little too much compared with the shots around it, so we softened the grass movement near him and rendered the clip again. Nothing else changed. In the final clip, every frame matches the still: Rustboy, the bird and the flake never change.

## Where it stands

The petrel scenes now run from the first meeting to the theft, the look down from the cliff top, the shelf partway down, and then the nest where the flake is given back. The climb between the cliff top and the shelf still isn't shown. Right after this shot comes the nest, which was already in the cut. In the nest scene that follows, he's lying on his back on wet rock below the nest. How he goes from standing on the shelf to lying there isn't in the film yet either; it's another gap the team may fill later.
