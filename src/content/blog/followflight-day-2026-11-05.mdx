Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is standing at the edge of a cliff on the headland, in gray light after the rain, with wind in the grass and salt mist hanging over the sea. A moment ago a storm-petrel, a small dark seabird, pecked a copper flake off his chest and flew away with it. There's a fresh bare spot beside the round port where the flake used to be. Now the bird has landed on a ledge partway down the cliff below him, the flake still in its beak. Rustboy has turned his head and is looking down at it.

Why that matters: something has just taken a piece of him, and his first move isn't to grab it back. It's curiosity. He watches where it went. That's what lets the scene that follows, at the bird's nest, be about care instead of about getting his property back.

## One new shot

There's one new shot in today's cut. Until now the film jumped straight from the petrel flying off with the flake to the nest ledge. This shot connects the two. In the cut it runs from about 3:01 to 3:07, right after the bird takes the flake and just before the nest.

## How we made it

Rustboy is built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks), used as drawn and never flipped. His body is one of the sheet's three-quarter views and his head is another, turned back over his shoulder toward the ledge. That head turn is the only acting in the shot. He doesn't lean, crouch or reach.

The petrel is the same bird from the shot before, so the two scenes match, now settled on the ledge with the flake. We matched the sky and light to the scenes on either side.

The team signed off on the look, and then we grew the clip from the still with only the mist and grass moving. Our quality check compared every frame against that still: Rustboy, the bird and the flake don't change at any point.

## What it sounds like

You hear the wind across the cliff top, the mist, and the sea far below, which you hear but never see move. The grass rustles in the wind. About halfway through, one distant, tentative note rises and fades, and nothing answers it, like a question left hanging. Then it settles into the quiet that opens the nest scene.
