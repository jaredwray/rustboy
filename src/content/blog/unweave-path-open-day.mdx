In a narrow rocky tide channel, foam climbs between dark wet rocks under a storm sky. Rustboy stands in the water. He is the leftover scrap kid with one green eye and a sealed rust face. In front of him a small dark seabird, a storm-petrel, is alive and caught in thin metal wire. He doesn't yank it free. He works his thin scrap fingers into the wire and unweaves it slowly, one loop at a time.

Then he turns to the fox. She has four legs and no wire on her, but a tangle of scrap stands between her and the high rock while the tide keeps rising. He pulls the scrap aside so she has a way up. All film long he has been looking for a purpose, and so far every answer has come up empty. Here help is something he does with his hands, slowly and carefully, standing in the tide for two animals that aren't his. The fox's way to high ground is open because he opened it. The rescue isn't finished yet. Her climb out and the moving shot of the path opening are still ahead.
