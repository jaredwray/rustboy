Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone at the bottom of a metal stair deep inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. A dead phone he found has just given him what he takes as his first job: *WAKE PAIR — BAY 3*. So he goes looking for Bay 3. Below the fabrication bay, the service level is flooded with dark, still water. A stencilled BAY 3 sign on the far wall is half sunk under the waterline. He stands on the last dry landing, and the only thing looking back at him is his own green eye, reflected in the flood. Then he turns and climbs back up toward the glass corridor.

Why that matters: he woke up with no purpose and he's hunting for one. The phone looked like an answer, someone needs you, and here's where. This is the moment he actually tries to do the job, and the place the job lives is gone under water. Nobody's there. Only his reflection answers. It's the first of his wrong answers landing, and it's what sends him up to the glass corridor to try the next one.

## How we made it

The background is new: a flooded service room in the same cold blue-grey steel look as the phone and glass-corridor scenes, with dark still water, a metal stair and landing, and the sign on the far wall.

The sign took three tries. The image generator wouldn't paint a sign half under water, so we hand-drew BAY 3 onto the wall. On the first try, the top of the B looked like an R. On the second, the B was fixed, but the waterline swallowed the fork of the Y, the crossbar of the A and the middle of the 3, so it read more like "BAV". The third try squeezed the letters so the strokes that make them readable sit above the water, and it now reads BAY 3 at normal size. Each fix touched only the sign, and our quality check confirmed no other pixel changed.

Rustboy himself is the approved pose from his character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks), placed into the background as drawn, never flipped, with his feet on dry metal above the water and a soft contact shadow under him. That's also why only his eye shows in the water and not his body: a body reflection would mean mirroring his image, and we never mirror him.

The shot passed QC (our frame-by-frame check against the character sheet) on character and colour the first time. The only rework was the sign. The clip was grown from the finished still, and the new shot went into the cut from the full-quality master copy of the clip rather than a compressed one.

## What it sounds like

Cold room tone, a slow lap of water that follows the ripple in the picture, and a few sparse drips. No music. Rustboy is standing still, so there are no footsteps or body sounds. The scene sits a little quieter than the phone scene before it, about -22.5 LUFS (a loudness measure), against about -20.6 LUFS for the whole cut. Everything else in the soundtrack is exactly as it was in the 20 October cut, just shifted later to make room.

## What's still soft

- The pose comes from an eye-level drawing on the character sheet, but the background looks down on him more steeply, so the angles don't quite match.
- His head is tilted a little to one side. It reads more like a cocked head than a look down at the water.
- The water's blacks are a little deep.
- In the clip, a wet patch under the bottom step is held still, so it reads as floor rather than water.
- The drips were timed to ripples that come from video compression, not the real water motion, so they play as drips somewhere off-screen in the room.
- He's small in the frame, about 299 pixels tall, and the background was enlarged 1.4x from its original size.
- Because the letters were squeezed above the waterline, the hand-drawn sign looks like a squat, wide cut of the font.
