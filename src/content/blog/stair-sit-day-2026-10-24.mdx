Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone on the spiral service stair that winds up through the tall well of light inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. Pale shafts of light fall from far above, and the stone below him is dark. Partway up, he sits down and stays there longer than he needs to, forearms over his knees, looking down at the steps he's already climbed. Nobody calls him. No job comes. Only the light and the dust in it move.

Why that matters: he woke up with no purpose and has been hunting for one, and every answer the keep offered came up empty: the phone's "job", the drowned Bay 3, the lever he wouldn't pull. The stair gives him nothing back either. This pause is where it sinks in that no one is coming to tell him what he's for. While he's still sitting, his power flickers, in the next shot, which was already in the film. He gets up anyway, only because he can, and climbs on toward the door of light.

## The new shot

It's one new shot, right after the well of light and right before his power flickers, at roughly 0:54 to 1:00 in the new cut. Nothing already in the film changed; everything after it just starts about six seconds later.

With this new shot, the order changes slightly from our last post: there, his power sagged and then he sat. Now he sits first, and the flicker finds him already sitting. Our story bible (the team's written outline of the film) still has the old order, and we've left that text as written for now; the post follows the cut. We tried the shot in both places. Placed after the flicker, the film went straight steps, then spiral stair, then straight steps again, which read as a cut to some other place. Placed right after the well of light, it stays on the same spiral stair he was already climbing, and the sitting runs straight into the flicker.

## How we made it

The background is a newly made spiral stair in the well of light, colour-matched to the shots around it. We didn't reuse the straight stair from the next shot, because a straight stair seen head-on would squash his seated legs.

We made three versions of the pose first, each leaning forward by a different amount, and picked the one framed larger and one step lower, where his face, chest port and feet read best. That version failed QC (our frame-by-frame check against his character sheet, the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) on one thing: where his near upper arm had been cut out of the sheet, a straight edge showed, so the arm looked like a flat sliver and lost its round shoulder. A second pass re-cut only that arm, same pose, same size, and it passed.

A few days ago a shot failed because his head was cocked on his neck. This time his whole upper body leans forward about 20° and his head stays level on his body, so he looks down the stair without a tilt. He's built from the character sheet as drawn, never flipped, with both feet visible. The final still passed on head angle, where he's looking, his eye and socket, chest port, feet and face. The clip passed too: its first frame matches the still exactly, and Rustboy, his shadow and his eye don't change in a single frame. No light touches him. We locked the look before it went into motion, and the new shot went into the cut from the full-quality master copy of the clip rather than a compressed one.

## What it sounds like

The hollow air and low hum of the well of light simply carry on into this shot and thin out slowly toward the next one. No music and no new sounds, and since he's sitting still, no footsteps or body sounds. It sits quieter than the shots on either side, about -24.6 LUFS (a loudness measure), against about -20.8 LUFS for the whole cut.

## What's still soft

- Only about three quarters of his round shoulder shows; the top tucks behind his chest plate, and it's low-contrast, so it blends a little into the step behind it.
- He leans forward about 20°, a touch more than a relaxed sit.
- His far forearm is hidden, and because his arms are short on the character sheet, his elbow doesn't quite reach his knee.
- The pose comes from an eye-level drawing, but the camera sits lower on the stair, so the angles don't quite match.
- The spiral stair was newly generated and then enlarged, so it's a little softer than a native image.
- In the clip, settling dust brightens slightly where it lands, and those spots look a touch flat.
- The jump from the spiral stair to the straight stair in the next shot was already in the film; this shot doesn't fix it.
