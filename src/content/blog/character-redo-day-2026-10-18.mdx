Rustboy is a small, rusted child-robot crossing a storm-beaten shore. On wet seaweed at the edge of the tide, a fox stands free on four legs about ten yards away. They see each other, and neither one closes the gap. Later they share the sheltered side of the same rocks, out of the wind, with air still between them. Further on, he reaches a tide channel where a petrel, a small seabird, is caught in scrap wire.

These moments are where the film shows that company doesn't have to mean owning something. They only work if Rustboy is the same robot in every frame. If he looks a little different from one scene to the next, a viewer stops believing it's the same kid making that walk.

## Why these scenes were redone twice

Yesterday we rebuilt five scenes where Rustboy had drifted from how he's supposed to look: the shore meeting with the fox, the lee of the rocks, a moment alone in the rain, a moment in tall grass, and the channel with the petrel. Watching them back, we saw they'd been made from the wrong version of him. It was close, but it wasn't the approved character. So we pulled those versions before they went up on the site.

Today all five were redone from his **character sheet**, the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks. Each one has a finished photo, a single locked frame that sets how the scene looks, and a short clip, a few seconds of motion grown from that photo.

## The new cut

The new progress cut, the single reel that lines up every finished shot in story order with the music, swaps in all five redone scenes and the crawl. Everything else is the same as the October 16 cut. It runs about 3:31.

To be clear about where this leaves the film: these five scenes now match the approved character sheet, but the rest of the film doesn't yet. Most of the stretch from the channel onward, where he steps in to help, frees the bird, climbs out and walks toward the horizon, still shows an older version of Rustboy. So do the nest scene just before the shore and three earlier shots. Those are next.

Our quality check counted 14 of the 27 shots with Rustboy in them as matching the sheet in this cut. That's progress, not a finished look, and his appearance visibly switches wherever a redone scene meets one that hasn't been redone yet.
