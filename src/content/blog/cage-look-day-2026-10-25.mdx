Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone in the lever room deep inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. On a raised control platform there's a dusty console, a few dying lamps, and a red safety lever locked under a wire cage. He comes up beside it, stands on the console deck right next to the cage, and leans in to peer down over it at the lever and the workings underneath. He doesn't reach for it. Only the room moves: a few lamps breathe brighter and dimmer, a faint haze drifts, and a soft glint slides along the cage.

Why that matters: he woke up with no purpose and has been trying every answer the keep offers. The dead phone gave him a "job" that went nowhere, and the light behind the glass didn't pair with him. The lever looks like the most important job in the building, but it was made for bigger hands that left long ago. He looks it over, takes it seriously, and leaves it alone. It's his first real choice of his own: he won't wear a larger hand. That idea comes back later in the film, when he almost scoops something out of a tide pool and stops.

## The new shot

It's one new shot, right after the closer shot of him at the base of the lever and right before the wall of staff badges, at roughly 0:42 to 0:48 in the new cut. Nothing already in the film changed; everything after it just starts about six seconds later.

One change from the outline: our story bible (the team's written outline of the film) has him climbing onto the cage to see over the lever. At his real size his feet don't fit the bar, so here he looks over it from the console beside it. We kept the bible text as written for now, the same as with last post's stair change; the post follows the cut.

## How we made it

The background is built from the earlier, closer lever shot's own picture, pulled back wider so it's the same lever and the same cage. We painted out the version of Rustboy already standing in that frame and filled in the new edges. Our very first background for this shot was made from scratch, but its lever was a different design from the one already in the film, so we switched.

Rustboy took several takes (a take is one attempt at the finished still). The first three failed on his arms: the arm nearest the camera was tucked behind his body so it looked missing, and a visible cut showed at the other elbow; one of those also had a lean that looked cocked. The next take fixed the arms but came out about 40% smaller than in the shot before, so he'd visibly shrink beside the same lever, and his eye sat below the lever's grip, so he wasn't seeing over anything. The take in the film puts him back at matching size with his eye just above the grip, and at that size he stands on the deck beside the cage.

We did try to keep the climb. A version with his hand on the cage rim was stopped before it was built, because lifting the arm would have shown the cut where the arm meets the shoulder. A step-up version, one foot on the cage bar and one on the deck, failed because the foot on the bar floated, touching only at the heel with a dark gap under the toe. Seating that foot properly would have meant reshaping his rounded boot to lie along the straight, sloped bar, and we don't reshape him. So we chose his true size over the climb.

He's built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) as drawn, never flipped or stretched. The final still passed QC (our frame-by-frame check against that sheet) on size, head angle, eye and socket, arms, hips, feet, face and keeping clear of the lever. The clip passed too: its first frame matches the still exactly, and Rustboy and the lever don't change in a single frame. We locked the look before it went into motion, and the new shot went into the cut from the full-quality master copy of the clip.

## What it sounds like

The lever room's quiet room tone carries straight on from the shot before. There's no music: the film's little searching tune is held back here on purpose, so the lever gets silence instead of a theme. A very soft electrical buzz rises and falls with the lamps. He's standing still, so there are no footstep or body sounds, and the glint on the cage makes no sound. It's about -25.6 LUFS (a loudness measure), quieter than the rest of the film, against about -20.9 LUFS for the whole cut.

## What's still soft

- He stands beside the cage, not on it, unlike the outline.
- His gaze lands on the lever's base and workings more than the red grip, about 31° off it. It reads as studying how the lever works, but he isn't looking at the handle itself, and his eye is only just above the grip.
- The cut from the close lever shot to this wide one makes him look suddenly small beside the console. He and the lever stay in the same proportion, so we kept it as a close-to-wide cut.
- His far shoulder is low-contrast and blends into the dark machinery behind it.
- The patch where the earlier Rustboy was painted out is a little softer than the rest, and the edges of the wider frame are a touch darker and cooler.
- In the clip, the glint shows up a bit later and stays a bit longer than planned.
