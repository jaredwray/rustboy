Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone on the halfway landing of a spiral service stair inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. A crack in the wall weeps a thin line of rain beside him. Light falls from above; the stair below him is dark. After seeing his whole first act in the puddle at his feet — the table, the phone, the lever, none of them an answer — he lifts his head toward the light and pushes himself up off the landing to stand. His green eye steadies, dimmer than before, but holding.

Why that matters: nothing in the puddle answered him. He stands anyway, because he can. That choice is what carries him on to the door of light at the top of the stair. The look-down into the puddle was already in the film; this is the other half of that mid-stair moment — the look up, and the stand.

## The new shot

It's one new shot, right after the puddle look-down and right before he reaches the threshold at the top of the stair, at roughly 1:37 to 1:43 in the new cut. Nothing already in the film is meant to change once this inserts; everything after it just starts a few seconds later. The stand-after-puddle shot is now in today's progress cut.

Our story bible (the team's written outline of the film) already writes this moment for the mid-stair landing: looks down, looks up, stands, eye steadies — dimmer but honest. This picture is that stand. No change to the outline.

## How we made it

The finished still is him standing eye-level on the landing, a small tip-back lean, green optic on the viewer-left side of his head, whole uncut limbs, split light across his plate, rain thread beside him — continuous with the sag landing and the puddle. He's built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) as drawn, never flipped or stretched: one green eye, dark empty socket, nose, mouth slit, chest port, rounded feet.

The still passed QC (our frame-by-frame check against that sheet). The clip passed too: its first frame matches the finished still exactly, and Rustboy — including his optic — doesn't change in a single frame; only the rain leak, rays, motes and a little paper or haze move. We locked the look and promoted it before it went into motion. The clip is grown from the finished still with environment-only motion: the room breathes while he stays still on purpose.

## What it sounds like

The puddle bed and a soft leak and ray shimmer continue through this stand. He's frozen, so there are no footstep or stand-up sounds. The film's little searching tune is held back on purpose — it waits for the threshold at the top of the stair. The new shot sits at about −26.5 LUFS (a loudness measure), against about −21.2 LUFS for the whole cut, with a true peak of −7.4 dBTP.
