Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone on the last flight of a spiral service stair inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. Above him, at the top of the stair, a square metal-framed hatch opens into a small stone hut, and pale gray daylight pours down through it into the dark well. He is on the steps below it, beside the iron rail, his eye turned up toward that square of light. Dust hangs in the beam, and a flake of rust drifts up into it ahead of him.

Why that matters: a moment earlier, on the halfway landing, he chose to stand. This is what that choice costs. He has less power than before, and the climb is slower, but he keeps going toward the light. At the top there is no maker and nobody waiting, only the hut and the door out into the weather. This shot is the last stretch of stair before he gets there.

## The new shot

Two days ago this scene was finished as a picture and a short clip but hadn't made it into the full film in time, so that day's post was a quiet one with nothing new to show. Now it's in. It sits right after he stands on the landing and right before the threshold, where we see him from behind stepping out through the doorway. That's roughly 1:43 to 1:49 in the new cut. Nothing else in the film changed; everything after it just starts six seconds later.

Without this shot the film jumped from Rustboy standing on the landing straight to Rustboy already in the doorway. Our story bible (the team's written outline of the film) has a line for it: the last flight is slower, rust flakes from his mismatched plate and rides the beam. This picture is that line.

## How we made it

The camera looks up the stair from below, so the hatch is a small bright square high in the frame and the curve of the rail leads your eye to it. Rustboy is small against all that stone, which is the point of the shot. He's built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) as drawn, never flipped: spherical rust head, one green eye, a dark empty socket, nose, mouth slit, chest port, rounded feet. His eye is kept as dim and steady as it was on the landing, so the two shots read as the same tired moment.

We locked the look of the still before any motion, then grew the clip from it with only the environment moving. Our quality check compared every frame of the shot against the character sheet. Rustboy's pixels didn't change across a single frame, and the one green eye stayed on the viewer's left the whole way through.

## What it sounds like

He's frozen, so there are no footsteps or rail sounds, and that's deliberate rather than missing. The film's little searching tune is held back here too; it's saved for the threshold. The new six seconds sit quiet, at about −26.2 LUFS (a loudness measure) against about −21.3 LUFS for the whole cut, so the jump in level when the threshold opens does some of the work. The loudest peak anywhere in the cut is −7.2 dBTP, well clear of clipping.

## What's still soft

- The version of the shot inside the full cut is a fresh encode from the same master as the clip above, not the identical file. They look the same; the numbers differ slightly.
- Some light and haze shimmer in the clip was accepted as part of the room, not treated as a mistake.
