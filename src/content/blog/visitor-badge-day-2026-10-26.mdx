Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone deep inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. The corridor is lined with years of peeling laminated staff IDs — some smiling, all dusted. He goes face by face along the wall, then stops and leans up toward a child's VISITOR badge. His green eye catches softly in the dusty plastic. Neither that child nor a kind technician nearby is him. He is not on the wall. Only the room moves: paper posters breathe a little, and a lamp glows and dims.

Why that matters: he woke up with no purpose and has been trying every answer the keep offers. The dead phone gave him a "job" that went nowhere, the light behind the glass didn't pair with him, and he left the big caged lever alone. The badges look like proof that someone belongs here — a role, a face that owns the job. A child's visitor pass is almost tender and almost cruel: someone small was welcomed once, and it still isn't him. He walks on without a badge of his own. Purpose is not a paper ID.

## How we made it

The finished still is him leaning up (a small look-up) at the child's VISITOR badge, green optic on the viewer-left side of his head, a soft green reflection in the dusty laminate, whole uncut limbs, head level with his body. He's built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) as drawn, never flipped or stretched: one green eye, dark empty socket, nose, mouth slit, chest port, rounded feet.

The still passed QC (our frame-by-frame check against that sheet) on size, head angle, eye and socket, arms, hips, feet, face details, and the lean-up read at the badge. The clip passed too: its first frame matches the finished still exactly, and Rustboy — including his optic — doesn't change in a single frame; only paper and lamp light move. We locked the look before it went into motion. The clip is grown from the finished still with environment-only motion: the room breathes while he stays still on purpose. The new shot went into the cut from the full-quality master copy of the clip.

## What it sounds like

The badges-corridor sound bed holds straight through this look. The film's little searching tune is held back on purpose — there is no match here, so no theme celebrating one. He's frozen, so there are no footstep or body sounds. A soft lamp buzz earlier in the corridor was retuned a little lower to clear a faint pre-echo before the badges. The new shot sits at about −24.2 LUFS (a loudness measure), against about −20.9 LUFS for the whole cut, with a true peak of −7.3 dBTP.
