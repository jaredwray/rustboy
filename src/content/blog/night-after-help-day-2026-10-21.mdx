It's the first night after Rustboy left the keep, the dead coastal fortress where he woke up. The small rusted robot, with his one green eye and one empty socket, stands alone on wet rock by a calm sea under a clear sky full of stars. The fox and the bird he helped are somewhere else tonight. He tilts his head up and lines his eye up with a single star, a little brighter than the rest.

He's done this before. Inside the keep he lined his eye up with a stubborn blinking server light, hoping it would answer him and tell him what he was for. It didn't. Nothing in the keep did, not the work order, the badges or the lever. Since then he's found his purpose by helping other lives, so tonight the star doesn't answer, and he doesn't need it to. The scene shows that change without a word, and then hands off to the morning walk along the horizon, which is still the film's last image.

## A new scene

This is a brand-new shot, the first one added since every scene in the film was brought back to the right Rustboy. It sits between him leaving the keep behind and the horizon morning. The film is still well short of its full length, so the team is now adding scenes like this one. Nothing already in the film changed.

## How it was made

The background is a night version of a stretch of shore the film already uses, the same coast as his quiet moment after the climb out. The team regraded it to night and gave it a real starfield, with one star slightly brighter than the others on his eye line. A fox that stood in the original background went out with the old sky, and the keep is out of frame.

Rustboy himself is the approved pose from his **character sheet**, the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks. He was placed into the scene as drawn, never flipped, with only a slight tilt of the head up toward the star and a soft shadow where he meets the rock.

The first try was held back by **QC** (quality control), the team's frame-by-frame check against the character sheet, for one thing: a thin dark sliver where his neck meets the night sky. The second try fixed only that edge and passed. The look was shown to the team lead before anything else was built on it.

When it went into the film, the night shot was rebuilt from the full-quality master copy of the clip rather than a compressed one, so his green eye stays as bright as it should.

## What it sounds like

Under the scene is a quiet bed of surf, low lapping water and light night wind, with one thin, low cello note. The film's four-note search melody is held back here on purpose, so the first time it plays is still the horizon morning.

## Honest flaws

There's no dune grass, only wet rock, because the coast backgrounds the film already uses don't have any. The night light cools his rust. He still reads as rust-brown with orange flecks, but he's warmer in the daylight shots. Because the camera sits low, the horizon lands at about his neck. He faces left, back toward where the keep is, so the star can sit on his eye line, and the walk away to the right picks up in the next shot. The image isn't mirrored.
