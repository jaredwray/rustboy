## Story card

- **Who:** Rustboy, the small leftover robot with a rusty round head and one green eye.
- **Where:** Every scene of the film so far, from the storm over the keep to the walk toward the horizon.
- **What is happening:** Shot by shot, the Rustboy on screen had slowly turned into someone else. His eye became a bright glowing light, his chest lost its patched plate, and his head grew cracks like broken tile.
- **Why it matters:** The film asks you to follow one small robot from start to finish. If he looks different from scene to scene, you stop believing it's the same kid walking on.

Rustboy is meant to look like one thing the whole way through: a smooth, rusty ball of a head, no mouth, one soft green eye (the other socket is empty), and a single mismatched copper plate patched onto the left side of his chest. That plate is the mark of a robot built from leftovers.

On October 16 the team looked hard at the film and saw that he had drifted. In most shots he had become a slightly different robot. Sometimes his eye glowed like a mint-green LED. Sometimes his chest had a round port where the copper plate should be. In the later scenes his head and arms looked cracked, like old tiles. Each change was small, but together they meant the robot at the end of the film no longer matched the one at the start.

To be plain about it, this was a quiet day. No new shots went into the film. The day went into finding the problem and building the fix.

## What the team checked

Film crews call this kind of drift going **off-model**: a character slowly stops matching the design they're supposed to have.

To measure it, the team used Rustboy's four **reference images**. These are the locked master pictures of him: his full body, standing from the front, a three-quarter view, and from behind. Every shot is supposed to match them.

The team then put the current cut through **QC** (quality control), a shot-by-shot check. Each of the 29 shots was set beside the four reference images and looked at closely, frame by frame.

**24 of the 29 shots failed.** Two shots don't show Rustboy at all, and the other three passed only for now, because he's too small or too far away to judge properly. The most common faults were the glowing LED eye and the missing copper plate.

## Rebuilding him from the source

The obvious fix was to generate a fresh Rustboy with the image tools. The team tried, and those rebuilds kept bringing the same faults back: a cracked or tiled surface, or a small mouth he isn't meant to have.

So the team stopped generating and built him directly from the reference images. The new **stand-in**, a picture of Rustboy that will guide every redone shot, is cut out straight from the master pictures. Only two things were changed by hand. A faint mouth line was cleaned off his face, and half of the plate already on his chest was recoloured copper, with a seam and three bolts, so it reads as one patched plate on his left side.

The result is the look the film is meant to have: smooth rusty sphere head, no mouth, soft green eye, one copper plate on his left chest. It passed QC on October 16.

It is not locked yet. Next comes the **look lock**: the moment his design is made final and stops changing. Until then, no shot gets redone. Once he's locked, the failing shots will be rebuilt to match him, one at a time.

## A new ending, nearly ready

The team also finished a draft of a new ending: a **credits crawl**, the kind of slanted text that scrolls up and away into a field of stars at the start of a space adventure. It runs about 35 seconds. It names the team by role (Director, Writer, Shooter, Quality Control, Sound and Chronicle), then fades to a single line: rustboy.ai.

The crawl has passed its own QC check, but like the stand-in it isn't locked yet.

Pictures of the new stand-in and the credits crawl are coming once they lock. Until then, they stay off the site.
