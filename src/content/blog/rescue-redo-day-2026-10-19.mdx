A rising tide fills a narrow rocky channel under a storm sky. A storm-petrel, a small seabird, is caught in scrap wire on the ledge, and a four-legged fox is cut off on the low rock. Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye, steps down into the water. He crouches over the bird, works the wire loose with his thin fingers, and drags aside the tangle blocking the fox. The fox scrambles up to high rock and the petrel lifts off over the surf. His back plate tears on the wire. He hauls himself out onto a low shelf, tired, and later walks on alone along a wet coast path toward brighter weather, still not fixed.

This is the heart of the film. He spends the story hunting for a purpose, and here he finds it in helping two lives he doesn't own. It costs him, and it doesn't fix him. For that to land, he has to look like the same robot here as everywhere else.

## Today's work

Most of the rescue had been made with an older version of Rustboy that had drifted from his approved design. On October 18 the team rebuilt it from his **hero design**, the locked master pictures that define exactly how he looks. That covered stepping into the water, unweaving the wire, opening the path, the release, the climb out and the walk at the horizon.

The team also fixed his face in three early scenes. In each one only his face was repainted, and the rest of the picture was left alone.

Each scene below has a finished photo, a single locked frame that sets how the scene looks, and a short clip, a few seconds of motion grown from that photo.

## What still doesn't match

Three scenes still show the older Rustboy in this cut: the nest scene before he meets the fox, his quiet moment with what's still missing after the climb out, and the shot where he leaves the keep behind. The nest and the quiet moment are being redone next. The keep-behind shot has already been redone and passed its quality check, but it won't join the film until the next cut. So his look still visibly switches in those places. It's progress, not a finished look.
