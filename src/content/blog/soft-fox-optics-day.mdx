On wet tidal seaweed, about ten yards of open air lie between Rustboy and a fox. Rustboy is the leftover scrap kid from the coastal keep: a rust sphere for a head, one green eye, a face that stays sealed. The fox stands free on four legs. They see each other. Neither closes the gap. This is the low point where a purpose still feels empty. After the nest, care without owning has a face — the fox is not a job and not a rescue yet. The meeting has to read as a real animal across real distance.

Later they share the same shore in the wind-shadow — the lee, the sheltered side of the rock. The fox stands in profile on four legs. Rustboy does not reach for it. Quiet company, air still between them, not a hug. That picture is fellowship as presence without owning, before the later absence and the walk to the horizon. The rising-tide rescue is still ahead and is not in this cut.

The team finished photos and short moving clips of both moments, then folded them into a new progress cut with the existing score. A finished photo is one locked frame. A short moving clip is a few seconds of motion grown from that frame. A progress cut is the single reel that lines finished shots up in story order and carries the music mix.
