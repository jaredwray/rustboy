On wet tidal rock, seaweed underfoot, about ten yards of open air lie between Rustboy and a fox. Rustboy is the leftover scrap kid from the coastal keep: a rust sphere for a head, one green eye, a face that stays sealed. The fox stands free on four legs. This is their first recognition. His eye reads warm — phosphor, a living green — so the meeting can feel alive. A cold toy light would make the same look fake. Fellowship starts here. He has spent the film hunting a purpose; the warmth keeps this living-world bond readable before the story later asks him to help.

After that first look they share the same coastal stone under an overcast sky. The lee is the wind-shadow, the sheltered side of the rock. They keep to that shelter, and the air between them stays open. Purpose is still empty. Nothing has asked him for help yet. He is learning how to be company while the fox remains her own animal. That quiet is the picture of fellowship, ahead of what follows: the empty eye socket beside an unfinished world, the keep small behind him, and the walk out along the horizon.
