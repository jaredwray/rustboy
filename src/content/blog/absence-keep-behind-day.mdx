The tide is draining. Rustboy is out of the water now. He's the leftover scrap kid with one green eye and a sealed rust face, and he sits alone on a flat wet rock shelf, knees bent, small and still, with his back to us. He raises one thin hand and touches the rim of his empty eye socket with two fingertips. He doesn't cover it or try to fill it. He just touches the edge. Far away, the fox stands on a distant rock on four legs. She's free and doesn't come to him.

He helped her, and he helped the storm-petrel caught in the wire. What he's missing is still missing. Helping other lives was where his purpose landed, but it didn't make him whole.

Then the storm is gone. Under a flat grey sky, he walks a wet rocky path beside the sea. Far behind him, small on a rise, sits the keep, the dead coastal fortress where he woke up with no purpose and went looking for a work order. He doesn't look back at it. The fox is far ahead on higher ground, going her own way, not at his heel. He keeps walking.

This isn't the end of the film. The walk toward the horizon is still being made.

## How the team made it

For each moment, the team finished one photo and a short moving clip grown from it. A finished photo is one locked frame that sets how a moment looks. A short clip is a few seconds of motion.

The photo of him on the rock shelf took more than one try. In an early try his head was turned the wrong way, which put the empty socket on the wrong side of his face, so the team set it aside. In the photo they kept, the socket is on the correct side, dark and empty, and his fingertips rest on the rim without plugging it.

The walking-away photo was built in two parts. The team liked the landscape in one take, with the coast path, the small far keep and the fox up ahead, but not the robot in it. So they made a new picture of Rustboy walking on his own, then placed him into that landscape. Afterward they painted out a tiny curl of wire that the old robot had left behind in the sky.

In both clips only the water moves: the sea, and the shine on the wet rock and path. Rustboy, the fox and the keep stay exactly as they are in the approved photos. The team keeps working this way because animating whole pictures kept making the robot stand up and turn toward the camera.
