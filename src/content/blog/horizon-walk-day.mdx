The squall has passed. On a wet rocky path beside a grey sea, Rustboy walks toward open weather. He's the leftover scrap kid with one green eye and a sealed rust face, and we see him from his left side, mid-stride, head level, looking ahead. The empty eye socket is a dark hole on the near side of his head. His back plate is still torn from the rescue.

Far ahead on her own stretch of coast, the four-legged fox goes her own way. She isn't at his heel, and she isn't coming back to him. Far out over the water, the storm-petrel he freed is only a tiny dark dash near the horizon.

He spent the whole film looking for a purpose. Helping other lives is where that purpose landed, and it still hasn't made him whole. He doesn't look back at the keep, the dead coastal fortress he woke up in, for a work order. He just keeps walking.

## How the team made it

The team finished one photo of this walk and a short moving clip grown from it. A finished photo is one locked frame that sets how a moment looks. A short clip is a few seconds of motion.

The team did not reuse the older shore-walk geography from before the rescue work. They built a new coast plate that matches the path after the climb-out and the walk away from the keep. One take had the landscape they wanted but not the robot. So they made a clean walking figure on its own, then placed him into that landscape — the same assemble approach used on the keep-behind walk. Soft notes stay in the captions; the story is the walk, the distance, and the habit of help.

In the clip only the water moves: the sea and a gentle shine on the wet path. Rustboy, the fox and the petrel stay exactly as they are in the approved photo. The team keeps working this way because animating whole pictures kept making the robot stand up and turn toward the camera.
