Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, has just looked back once at the keep. Now he's out on the open coastal trail under a heavy gray sky, with wind and salt mist coming in off the sea. A light drizzle is starting. He has his hand raised, the same hand he once pressed against the storm glass back in the server gallery, and the first raindrops sit beaded on his metal fingers.

Why that matters: early in the film he stood at that rain-streaked window with his palm flat on the glass and the storm on the other side. The glass kept the weather off him. Out here there's nothing between him and the world, and for the first time the rain actually touches him. It's only a drizzle in this shot. It builds into the full rain of the next one.

## One new shot

There's one new shot in today's cut. Until now the film went straight from his look back at the keep to Rustboy already standing in full rain, with drops hitting his chest. Nobody saw the moment the weather first reached him. This shot fills that gap. In the cut it runs from about 2:19 to 2:25, right after the look back and just before the rain.

## How we made it

The approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) has no drawing of an open hand turned palm-up to catch rain. Rather than invent one, we kept his real hand, as drawn, and let the drops land on his fingers. His green eye stays on the viewer's left and his empty socket on the other side, as always.

The drops took two tries. In the first version they were too bright, too round and too evenly placed, so they looked like metal studs on his hand. We remade them as water: see-through, softer, darker in the middle and scattered unevenly, so they read as beads of rain sitting on rust.

The rain needed a fix too. The motion is grown around Rustboy without touching him, and in an early version that left a dry patch around him for the first part of the shot, as if he had his own little bubble of clear air. We redid it so the drizzle is all around him from the first moving frame.

As with the last few shots, we locked the look of the still first, then grew the clip from it. Our quality check compared every frame against that still, and Rustboy's body, hand and eye don't change at any point in the clip.
