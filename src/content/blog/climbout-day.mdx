The squall is easing, and the water in the narrow tide channel has stopped climbing. At the channel's edge, where a low flat rock shelf meets the water, Rustboy hauls himself out. He's the leftover scrap kid with one green eye and a sealed rust face, and he comes up slow and tired, on one knee and one thin hand. The back plate he tore during the rescue is still torn, and a scrap of wire is still hooked in the edge.

Up on the high rock, the fox stands on four legs, free. She watches him but doesn't come down to him. The storm-petrel he unwove from the wire is only a tiny dark dash far out over the water.

He has spent the whole film looking for a purpose, and here helping other lives has actually happened. That doesn't mean he's fixed. The tear stays. The fox and the bird are free, and they don't belong to him. He doesn't look back at the keep, the dead coastal fortress he woke up in, for a work order. This isn't the end, either. The rest of the ending is still being made: his quiet moment with what's still missing, leaving the keep behind, and the walk toward the horizon.

## How the team made it

The team finished one photo of the climb-out and a short moving clip grown from it. A finished photo is one locked frame, and a short clip is a few seconds of motion.

The first take of the photo got the most important thing right. He's low, mid-haul, seen from behind, head down. That's the pose the team wanted, and it avoided the problem that kept spoiling earlier tries, where the robot stood up and stared at the camera. So instead of generating the picture again, the team did a small paint-in, which means repainting only a few small patches of that one image. One patch carried the torn plate edge and its hooked wire over from the rescue scene so the damage matches. Another added the distant bird as a dash over the sea. Everything outside those patches stayed exactly as it was.

In the clip, only the water moves. Rustboy, the fox and the far-off bird stay exactly as they are in the approved photo. The team keeps working this way on the rescue scenes because animating the whole picture kept making the robot stand up and turn to the camera.
