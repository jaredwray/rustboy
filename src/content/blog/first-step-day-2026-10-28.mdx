Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone deep inside the keep, the dead coastal data fortress where he woke up. This time he is no longer at the bottom of the shaft staring up. His left foot is down on the lowest tread of a tall spiral service stair, a stone-and-steel shaft of pale god rays and drifting rust motes. The film calls this place the well of light; it is not a water well. The camera has moved in closer than the look-up. The stair still rises tall around him, but now he is on it. Only the room moves: the god rays shimmer a little, and dust motes drift.

Why that matters: he woke up with no purpose and has been trying every answer the keep offers. The look-up was the hope that purpose might be waiting above. The planted foot is the choice to climb toward the door of light anyway, before the later mid-stair sag teaches him that no assignment is coming to meet him. He takes the stairs.

## How we made it

In the finished still his left foot is on the first tread and he leans back a little. His green optic is on the viewer-left side of his head, and his limbs are whole and uncut. He reads as solid rather than skinny. The frame is closer and lower than the look-up so the foot plant reads. He's built from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks) as drawn, never flipped or stretched: one green eye, dark empty socket, nose, mouth slit, chest port, rounded feet.

The still passed QC (our frame-by-frame check against that sheet) on size, head angle, eye and socket, arms, hips, feet, face details, and the read of the first step. The clip passed too. Its first frame matches the finished still exactly, and Rustboy, optic included, doesn't change in a single frame; only rays, motes and a little paper or haze move. We locked the look and promoted it before it went into motion. The clip uses environment-only motion: the room breathes while he stays still on purpose.

## What it sounds like

Those wait for the climb that's already in the film.
