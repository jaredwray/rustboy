Rustboy, the small rusted robot with one green eye and one empty socket, is alone on the headland, standing in coastal grass flattened by the wind. The rain has passed, and the light is gray with salt mist. On a wet rock an arm's length away sits a storm-petrel, a small dark seabird. It's studying him, or more exactly the rust-copper rim of the round port in the middle of his chest. He has turned his head toward it. Neither of them moves.

Why that matters: this is the first time they meet. Everything in the keep, the old fortress where he woke, ignored him, and here something living has noticed him first. He doesn't reach for it, and it doesn't fly. In the very next shot the bird pecks a copper flake from his chest and takes it, but before that there's this: a moment where each of them simply lets the other stay.

## One new shot

There's one new shot in today's cut. Until now the film went straight from Rustboy in the wind-blown grass to the petrel taking the flake, with no setup. The bird was simply there. This shot shows how they come face to face. In the cut it runs from about 2:49 to 2:55, right after the grass and just before the bird takes the flake.

## How we made it

Rustboy comes from the approved character sheet (the master set of pictures that defines exactly how he looks), used as drawn and never flipped. His body faces the camera, standing with his hands at his sides, and his head is the sheet's own three-quarter view, tilted slightly at the neck toward the bird. That turn is the only acting in the shot. We didn't invent a crouch or a reach. In this view his green eye is the far one, on the viewer's left, and the empty socket nearer to us still has the small white glint the sheet draws there.

The petrel is the same bird the film already has in the next shot, so that the two shots match. We placed it so it reads as already settled on the rock, not caught mid-landing, with its bill pointed at the rim of his chest port.

The team signed off on the look together, and then we grew the clip from the still with only the surroundings moving. Our quality check compared every frame against that still: Rustboy, the bird and the rock they're on don't change at any point.

## What it sounds like

The music doesn't start anything new here. The last breath of the grass scene's music, its wind and its low held note, carries on under the meeting while the sea washes underneath. Nothing new is played and nothing gets answered. Neither of them moves, so there are no footsteps or wingbeats either. Near the end the held note thins away, and the next shot opens bare, as it always has.
